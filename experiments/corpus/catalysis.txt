Heterogeneous Catalysis Notes

The rate of a heterogeneous reaction depends on the fraction of surface atoms
that are active sites. Turnover frequency, defined as molecules converted per
active site per second, separates intrinsic activity from surface area.

Sabatier's principle states that a catalyst is best when it binds the reactant
and the product neither too strongly nor too weakly. Binding too weakly leaves
the activation barrier intact; binding too strongly poisons the site and stops
the cycle.

The Langmuir Hinshelwood mechanism assumes both reactants adsorb before
reacting, and is distinguished from the Eley Rideal mechanism, in which a gas
phase species collides directly with an adsorbed species.

Deactivation of a catalyst occurs by coking, sintering, poisoning by sulfur or
lead, or by hydrothermal degradation. Sintering is driven by the migration of
small particles that merge into larger ones, reducing surface area irreversibly.
