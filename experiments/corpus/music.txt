Basso Continuo Practice

The basso continuo was the bass line plus a set of figures that directed the
harpsichordist's chordal filling. Figures were shorthand rather than full
harmony, and their meaning was conventional within a region rather than fixed.

A cadential six four was written as a bass note with a six and a four above it,
and functioned as a dominant embellishment rather than a stable chord, delaying
the cadence that follows it.

The rule of the octave was a practical guide for harmonising a scale descending
or ascending, assigning consonant intervals on strong beats and dissonant
intervals on weak ones.

Basso continuo practice implies a flexible disposition of tempo and
ornamentation, so a modern performance should treat the figures as a floor for
the harpsichordist rather than a ceiling on what is permitted.
